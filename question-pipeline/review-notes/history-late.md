## English: content doubts

- q_b140_027: 1 Kings 4:26 says 40,000 stalls; 2 Chronicles 9:25 says 4,000. I kept 4,000 out of the distractors, but a player who knows Chronicles may object.
- q_b140_044: 1 Kings 7:14 says Hiram's mother was of Naphtali; 2 Chronicles 2:14 says Dan (q_b145_003 asks the Chronicles version). Each question leaves the other tribe out of its distractors.
- q_b140_048: "Aaron's rod and the tablets" is a distractor. It is wrong per 1 Kings 8:9 ("nothing in the ark except the two stone tablets"), but Hebrews 9:4 lists the rod. Probably fine because the question cites 1 Kings.
- q_b142_020: WEB has "a little room on the wall"; many Bibles (and Luther) have "on the roof" (Obergemach). The answer text follows WEB.
- q_b142_049: 2 Kings 8:15 doesn't name the subject ("he took a thick cloth…"). The usual reading, and the next clause, is Hazael.
- q_b142_041: WEB spells "Benhadad" in 2 Kings 6:24 and "Ben Hadad" elsewhere. The answer uses "Ben Hadad".
- q_b143_018: the leper king is called Azariah in 2 Kings 15 and Uzziah in 2 Chronicles 26. Uzziah is left out of the distractors. The cited verse 15:5 doesn't name him; the context (15:1–3) does.
- q_b143_027: the answer is the one-word name "So" (king of Egypt). It may look odd on a card.
- q_b143_034: 2 Kings 18:31 (vine and fig tree) is also in Isaiah 36:16. If the Isaiah writer asked the same thing, verify.mjs may flag a near-duplicate.
- q_b143_043: WEB 2 Kings 20:12 spells "Berodach Baladan" (Isaiah 39:1 has "Merodach"). Translations use their Bible's form.
- q_b143_039: WEB spells "Esar Haddon" with a space.
- q_b143_055 / q_b146_042: WEB spells the Pharaoh "Necoh" in 2 Kings and "Neco" in 2 Chronicles. Each answer follows its own book.
- q_b144_022: 1 Chronicles 13:9 says "Chidon"; 2 Samuel 6:6 says "Nacon", which is left out of the distractors. (The earlier distractor "Goren", Hebrew for "threshing floor", was replaced with "Gilgal".)
- q_b144_032: 1 Chronicles 20:5 (Elhanan killed Lahmi, Goliath's brother) differs from 2 Samuel 21:19. The question says "According to 1 Chronicles".
- q_b144_034: 1 Chronicles 21:12 has three years of famine; 2 Samuel 24:13 has seven. "Seven years" is deliberately not a distractor.
- q_b145_014: the wording "such as had never been given before" paraphrases 2 Chronicles 9:9 ("neither was there any such spice").
- q_b147_003: some readers identify Sheshbazzar with Zerubbabel, so Zerubbabel is not used as a distractor.
- q_b147_006: Ezra 2:2's list also contains a Nehemiah and a Mordecai (different men from the famous ones). Both are distractors; they are wrong only because the question asks who is named *first*.
- q_b147_022: Zechariah is "son of Iddo" in Ezra 5:1 but "son of Berechiah, son of Iddo" in Zechariah 1:1, so Berechiah is not a distractor.
- q_b148_047: "Jeshua the son of Nun" is Joshua. Translations use Josua / Józsué.
- q_b149_014: the distractor "The Amalekite king" might be argued, since Agag was an Amalekite king. Haman himself is only called "the Agagite".
- q_b141_075: the answer "400" stands for "about four hundred men" in the text.
- q_b144_016: the answer is a phrase ("Both the right and the left"); the text is about slinging stones and shooting arrows.
